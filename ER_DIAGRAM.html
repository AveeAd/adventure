ActivityType nests via a self-referencing parentId (unbounded depth); name/slug stay globally unique regardless of nesting, and since Postgres can't enforce "not your own ancestor" as a constraint, the service layer must reject any update that would create a cycle. The location hierarchy replaces what was originally a flat Region table with real administrative geography; each level's slug is unique only within its parent, not globally. Both self-referencing and hierarchical relations use onDelete: Restrict rather than cascade — deleting a parent shouldn't be able to silently wipe everything beneath it. Every table above also carries isActive: "delete" in the admin dashboard sets it to false rather than removing the row.

The Content zone is the only place lines are drawn cross-table on purpose within a zone — everywhere else it references (ActivityType, DifficultyLevel, District, Season, User) live in other zones, shown as → table.column captions instead of long cross-zone lines, to keep the diagram legible. PageRevision stores a full Markdown snapshot per edit, never a diff — reverting is just copying an old snapshot into a new revision, and a "changes" view diffs two snapshots at render time rather than storing one. PageConfirmation ties to a specific revision, not the page itself, so an edit can't ride on confirmations that predate it — every new revision starts back at zero.

Map/geodata has no revision history, unlike PageRevision — Trail/Spot are edited in place, tracked only by createdById/lastEditedById. Since TrailConfirmation/SpotConfirmation have no revision to scope to, they tie directly to the row — which means the service layer, not the schema, is what keeps trust meaningful: any edit to a trail/spot's geometry must reset verificationStatus and delete its existing confirmations in the same transaction, or a moved trail could still show "verified" from confirmations that applied to its old path. SpotType lives in the Master data zone above, not here — it's a lookup table, not geodata itself.
